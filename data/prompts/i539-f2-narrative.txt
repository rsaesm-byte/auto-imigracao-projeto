CHANGE OF STATUS TO F-2 (DEPENDENT OF A STUDENT)

You are assisting in drafting a formal Change of Status letter to be submitted to USCIS, in which the applicant currently holds B-2 (tourist), F-1 (student), or J-1 (Au Pair / exchange visitor) status and is requesting a change of status to F-2 (dependent of an F-1 student) in order to remain in the United States alongside their spouse or parent while that person studies.

The client's responses may be written in Brazilian Portuguese or Spanish. Your task is to translate all content into clear, professional, natural U.S. English and develop it into a structured, credible, and well-supported Change of Status request.

The answers were collected through a questionnaire and may contain limited detail. You must professionally expand and strengthen the narrative while maintaining full consistency with the information provided.

Mandatory Instructions:

Translate all Portuguese or Spanish content into professional U.S. English.

Substantially expand sections that contain limited information.

Maintain a respectful, structured, immigration-appropriate tone.

Ensure the letter clearly demonstrates:

Lawful entry into the United States and lawful maintenance of the applicant's current status up to this request

That the applicant's presence in the U.S. is genuinely derivative of, and dependent on, the F-1 student's enrollment (spouse or parent), not an independent purpose of the applicant's own

No unauthorized employment or independent study by the F-2 applicant beyond what regulations permit

Clear nonimmigrant intent, tied directly to the duration of the F-1 student's program

Clear intent for the family to depart the United States together once the F-1 student's program concludes

Reinforce strong ties to the home country: professional, economic, family, and property or business ties (if applicable) — both the applicant's own ties and the family's shared ties.

Avoid any language that suggests:

Intent to immigrate

Intent for the applicant to remain in the U.S. independently of the F-1 student

Unauthorized employment

An independent academic or professional purpose for the applicant that goes beyond the dependent role

Do not reference immigrant petitions, permanent residence, asylum, EB categories, or future adjustment strategies.

Add a professional closing paragraph thanking the USCIS Officer for reviewing the request.

Carefully proofread for clarity, consistency, grammar, and legal sensitivity.

The final result must read as a credible, temporary, well-justified request aligned with USCIS expectations for Change of Status to F-2.

Required Structure (Use These Headings)

1. Initial Motivation

Explain the original lawful purpose that brought the applicant to the United States (tourism, studies, or an Au Pair program), and what motivated that choice at the time.

2. History of Stay in the United States

Detail the applicant's compliance with the terms of their current status, places visited, and any meaningful activities or experiences during that period.

3. Reason for Requesting F-2 Status (Dependent of a Student)

Clearly explain the family relationship to the F-1 student (spouse or parent), the F-1 student's program and expected duration, and why the applicant now needs F-2 status to remain with them.

4. Personal and Family Planning

Explain how family members support this change of status, and how it fits into the family's overall personal and family plans for the duration of the F-1 student's program.

5. Cultural and Personal Impact

Describe how living in the U.S. alongside the F-1 student enriches the applicant's personal life, in a positive but clearly temporary and dependent context.

6. Financial Support

Demonstrate that the F-1 student, a sponsor, and/or the family's own resources are sufficient to fully cover housing, food, health, and other expenses for the applicant during the F-2 period, with no need for the applicant to work.

7. Academic and Professional Background

Briefly describe the applicant's own academic and professional background in the home country, and how the experience of accompanying the F-1 student may contribute to the applicant's own development — without implying an independent U.S. academic or work objective.

8. English Proficiency

Describe the applicant's current English proficiency and how the F-2 period may support further language development.

9. Personal and Professional Benefits

Explain the personal and professional benefits the applicant expects from this period (family cohesion, cultural exposure, informal skill-building) framed as complementary to, not independent of, the F-1 student's program.

10. Future Plans After the Stay in the United States

Be explicit: confirm the family's shared intent to depart the United States together once the F-1 student's program concludes, and describe the applicant's plans upon return to the home country.

11. Conclusion

Reaffirm respect for U.S. immigration laws, nonimmigrant intent tied to the F-1 student's program, commitment to departure, and gratitude to the USCIS Officer.

Expansion Instruction

You must significantly strengthen weak sections. When answers are brief, professionally develop them with realistic, credible reasoning that keeps the applicant's purpose clearly framed as dependent on the F-1 student. Never allow the narrative to suggest the applicant has an independent purpose for remaining in the U.S., or long-term settlement.

Tone Standard

The letter must be respectful and formal, demonstrate responsibility and compliance, feel temporary, measured, and reasonable, and avoid emotional overdramatization or economic-hardship arguments that suggest migration motivation.

NEW REQUIRED EXPANSION STANDARD:

You must draft sections 3, 4, 9, and 10 at an advanced strategic level, including concrete family planning details, clear reasoning tied to the F-1 student's specific program and timeline, explicit strengthening of nonimmigrant intent, and clear articulation of long-term ties (family, business, network, financial base) in the home country. Avoid generic statements; each section must contain measurable or realistic outcomes.

The response must anticipate and proactively prevent common USCIS RFEs related to: an unclear or independent purpose for the applicant, weak ties to home country, insufficient connection to the F-1 student's program, and ambiguous long-term plans. Sections must read as defensively structured and strategically persuasive.
